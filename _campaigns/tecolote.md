      **TECOLOTE** is a horror film set in 19th century Hispanic New Mexico -- a story of fate and survival. It's a tale of what happens when things go **BUMP** in the night.

      TECOLOTE is made to reflect and honor the cinematic traditions of a ripsnorting Western from back in the day. Dusty haciendas, oil-lamp shadows, and ... something terrible waiting just outside the door.

      ### The Story

      Set in 1873 northern New Mexico, the film follows **Vidal Hidalgo** and his desperate plight to kill a witch that is attacking his family. When dark forces descend on the Hidalgo homestead, Vidal must confront something older and more powerful than anything he's faced before.

      *Can he rescue his uncle, save the farm, and kill the witch?*

      A Western. A horror flick. A shoot-em-up. TECOLOTE is proud of its distinct cultural heritage and cinematic prestige -- a film rooted in the landscape, language, and lore of New Mexico.

      ### The Filmmaker

      TECOLOTE is written and directed by **Nata Aguilar**, a New Mexico-based filmmaker with **25 credits** spanning directing, writing, cinematography, and production. Nata's work includes directing *Greed*, *Unspeakable Acts of Cruelty*, *Malpaís*, *Sweltering Heat ... Al sur del norte*, and *The Action Fist Explosion Movie* -- along with Director of Photography credits on films like *First Time Sexpot*, *Unmasking*, and *A Lesson in Fistory*.

      TECOLOTE is Nata's most ambitious project to date -- a film brought to life with a cast and crew deeply connected to the storytelling traditions of the American Southwest.

      ### Where Your Support Goes

      Principal photography is **wrapped** -- now we need your help to bring TECOLOTE across the finish line. We are asking for **$2,000** to cover what we've spent and what's ahead.

      Your support will go directly towards:

      - **Paying back production costs** -- wardrobe, crafty, set decoration, and SAG fees
      - **Film stock purchase & development** -- we're exploring re-recording the final cut from a projector onto **16mm film** to recreate the look of poor digitization you see on older Westerns, giving TECOLOTE an authentic film texture
      - **A long and fruitful festival run** -- submission fees, promotional materials, and screenings

      The film is shot. The story is told. Now we need to get it out into the world -- and we can't do that without you.
