## Owner Decisions and Boundaries

- The default policy is no returns or exchanges after a successful pledge/product charge.
- Seven calendar days is an ordinary fulfillment-problem reporting guideline measured from the carrier's delivered timestamp; it is not a return window and is not emitted as `merchantReturnDays`.
- The operator and seller identity derive from `platform.company_name`, falling back to top-level `author`; the support contact derives from `platform.support_email`.
- Only the existing featured tier can become a Shopping product, and it must be a physical reward with complete facts. Digital rewards, services, creative participation, campaign pages, and incomplete preorder records do not emit Product/Offer data.
- The Their Love poster is the current candidate, but `shopping.enabled` remains false. Confirming its exact availability date and completing Merchant Center/feed/destination setup are Future Features. The existing “January 2027” campaign timeline is not silently converted into an invented day.
- Merchant Center account creation, website verification, feed setup, destination configuration, Google review, and Shopping-tab placement are not claimed by this release.
- The generic Search Console “Something went wrong” interface error is deferred at owner direction. It is not treated as a product defect or a successful live test.
- The Spanish copy targets neutral US/Latin American Spanish. Automated completeness passed, and the owner confirmed completion of the final fluent review on 2026-07-14.

## Sitemap Investigation and Controls

The pre-release production endpoint returned HTTP 200, `application/xml`, valid XML, canonical Pool URLs, and the same body for ordinary, Googlebot, and Google Inspection user agents. `robots.txt` advertised the canonical sitemap. Search Console nevertheless reported “Couldn't fetch” / “Sitemap could not be read.” No reproducible origin denial or invalid response was found from direct requests.

One concrete sitemap defect was found: pages without authored dates inherited `site.time`, so every build claimed all URLs had changed. v1.1.2 removes that fallback. The generated audit now validates the XML declaration, BOM absence, parsing, canonical URLs, duplicate URLs, private-route exclusion, and valid nonfuture `lastmod` values.

Both production deploy workflows now run a dependency-free live audit after propagation. It:

- requests `/sitemap.xml` as an ordinary client and Google Inspection client
- requires identical valid XML bodies with a 200 response and XML MIME type
- validates the canonical `robots.txt` sitemap directive
- requests every sitemap URL as Google Inspection and requires a real canonical HTML document
- distinguishes Cloudflare's non-blocking JavaScript-detection injection from a blocking challenge/interstitial
- retries boundedly for deployment propagation, then fails the deploy evidence if the crawl surface remains invalid

After deployment, submit the canonical `https://pool.dustwave.xyz/sitemap.xml` sitemap again in the existing URL-prefix property and use Search Console's sitemap report. The URL Inspection tool's page-index status is not a sitemap parser test.

## Fulfillment-Problem Verification

For a tracked shipment, support records the pledge/order reference, report email timestamp, carrier, tracking number, carrier-delivered timestamp, fulfillment record, description, and available item/package/label photos. The seven-day guideline is the calendar-day difference between the carrier-delivered timestamp and initial report timestamp.

If tracking or a reliable delivered timestamp does not exist, support reviews the report timing and evidence in good faith rather than fabricating a delivery date or automatically denying the report. Available remedies are repair, replacement, completion of missing items, or refund of the affected item depending on the verified problem and inventory. Rights that cannot legally be waived remain unaffected.
